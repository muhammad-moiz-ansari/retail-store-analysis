\chapter{Introduction and Project Vision}
\label{ch:intro}
\section{Project Overview}
RetailPulse (\textit{Agentic AI for Retail Store Intelligence}) is a system that converts existing in-store CCTV footage into live customer-engagement analytics, staff-performance insight, and store-traffic intelligence, without new cameras or specialised hardware. It is a Final Year Project that explores practical use of multi-agent AI systems in a resource-constrained retail environment.

The system assumes a three-camera setup: one entrance-facing camera and two floor-facing cameras. Their feeds pass through a pipeline of specialised agents. An orchestrator reconciles the agents' outputs against the same tracked entities, and the result is shown on a web dashboard for managers and a workspace for employees. A separate POS analytics module covers sales and product trends.

\section{Problem Statement}
Retail store owners and managers have no low-cost way to know whether customers on the floor are being attended to, how staff time is split between active engagement and idle time, and which areas of the store attract traffic and which are ignored. Existing CCTV footage is recorded but rarely reviewed, and dedicated retail-analytics hardware or analysts are often financially out of reach for small and mid-sized retailers.

\section{Problem Context and Motivation}
CCTV systems in most stores exist for theft prevention. The footage holds operational information (queues, approaches, dwell, idle time) that goes unused. Managers who want this information must watch footage manually, which does not scale, or buy specialised products.

The motivation is threefold. First, service quality: customers who are not approached in a reasonable time may leave without buying. Second, fair and informed staffing: managers need visibility into floor coverage and idle periods. Third, accessibility: a CPU-only solution built on existing cameras lowers the barrier for retailers that cannot invest in new capital equipment. The project also emphasises privacy-conscious design, using non-biometric classification and presenting results as decision support rather than automated employee evaluation.

\section{Project Vision}
RetailPulse aims to make store intelligence accessible to retailers of any size by reusing the cameras they already own, running on commodity CPUs, and giving managers clear and trustworthy answers to everyday operational questions.

\section{Project Objectives}
The project has the following measurable objectives.
\begin{enumerate}[label=O\arabic*.]
\item Count footfall from the entrance camera and report it per time interval.
\item Detect people in each processed frame and classify them as staff or customer without biometric identification; the floor-distance prototype reuses a trained employee/customer detector, while earlier demo paths use zone and appearance heuristics.
\item Compute engagement metrics: approach time, interaction duration, idle time, unattended-customer flags, and an employee score.
\item Track movement to produce paths, zone dwell times, heatmaps, and crowd density.
\item Reconcile all agent outputs per tracked entity through an orchestrator.
\item Provide a manager dashboard, an employee workspace, alerts, and exportable reports with narrative summaries.
\item Provide POS analytics: CSV/XLSX import, product performance, purchase conversion, forecasts, recommendations, and cited assistant answers.
\item Demonstrate an experimental camera-handoff pipeline that associates a person leaving one camera with one entering another.
\item Estimate supported person positions and pair separations in a calibrated floor region, retaining explicit uncertainty and rejection states.

\item Run the vision pipeline on CPU only.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Project Scope}
\textbf{In scope:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Footfall counting (entrance camera).
\item Staff/customer classification (non-biometric, including the trained role detector used by the distance prototype).
\item Engagement analytics: approach time, interaction duration, idle detection, unattended flagging, and employee scoring.
\item Trajectory and spatial behaviour: path tracking, zone dwell time, heatmaps, and crowd density.
\item Homography-based floor-plane localization and provisional pair-distance estimation through local command-line tools.
\item POS CSV/XLSX import, product performance, purchase conversion, 7- and 30-day demand forecasts, inventory recommendations, and cited POS assistant answers for a single local store.
\item Web application with manager and employee roles.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Explicitly out of scope} (see Chapter~\ref{ch:fyp2plan} and Future Work):
\begin{itemize}
\item Multi-camera identity linking as full cross-camera re-identification (only an experimental two-camera handoff is included).
\item Full three-dimensional reconstruction and per-pixel depth-map generation; the implemented distance prototype is restricted to an approximately planar floor.

\item Shelf-level product interaction detection.
\item Long-term store layout and promotional-effectiveness evaluation.
\item Multi-store scaling, a live POS connector, and real-time processing as the primary deliverable (the current pipeline is offline/batch).
\end{itemize}
These exclusions were chosen after a feasibility triage against hardware (no dedicated GPU), timeline, and dependency constraints.

\section{Constraints and Assumptions}
\textbf{Constraints.} No dedicated GPU is available. The timeline is limited to the two FYP semesters. Only two supplied camera videos are currently available for the handoff experiment. Processing is offline/batch rather than real time.

\textbf{Assumptions.} Cameras are fixed, and the store has an entrance-facing camera and two floor-facing cameras. Camera views overlap or adjoin so that people leaving one view enter another within a bounded time window. Staff are distinguishable by zone and appearance cues (for example, working areas and uniforms). POS data can be exported as CSV or XLSX.

\textbf{Floor-distance assumptions and current status.} The operational floor is approximately planar, calibration points correspond to the same floor surface, and supported person boxes provide usable floor-contact proxies. Metric scale must come from supplied floor dimensions rather than a global image-space multiplier. The temporary \path{queue_temp.mp4} recording contains camera movement; its estimated calibration is provisional and all distance outputs are approximate. Unsupported camera alignment, occluded or truncated positions, and points outside the calibrated region are rejected. The prototype estimates two-dimensional floor separation, not full 3D depth.

\section{Ethical, Legal and DEI Considerations}
\textbf{Privacy.} The system does not use face recognition or any other biometric identification. The floor-distance prototype uses learned appearance-based role labels without facial identity storage; earlier demo classification paths use zone/appearance heuristics. Cross-camera matching uses coarse HSV and shape descriptors that are treated as evidence, not proof. Runtime footage and real calibration data remain local and are excluded from Git. Uncertain and unmatched cases are preserved, not forced into a match.

\textbf{Fair use of results.} The system is designed for manager decision support rather than automated employee evaluation. Employee scores are indicators for discussion, and the limitations of the heuristics must be communicated to users.

\textbf{Legal and consent.} Deployment in a real store requires signage and consent consistent with applicable law and store policy. At the time of writing, Pakistan did not have a fully enacted comprehensive data-protection statute, so the project follows data-minimisation principles consistent with widely used frameworks such as the EU GDPR \cite{gdpr2016}. \todo{confirm current legal position before final report}

\textbf{Accessibility and inclusion.} The web interface uses readable fonts, clear contrast, and charts with textual values. Appearance-based classification can perform unevenly across clothing styles, and this is recorded as a fairness-related limitation to evaluate in FYP-2.

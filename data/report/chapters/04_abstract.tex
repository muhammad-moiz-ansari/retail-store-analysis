\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Most retail CCTV installations are used only for theft prevention, so the footage holds little operational value. Small and mid-sized retailers in particular cannot afford specialised analytics hardware or dedicated analysts, and therefore cannot tell whether customers are being attended to, how staff time is divided between engagement and idleness, or which store zones attract traffic and which are neglected. This report presents \textit{RetailPulse}, a system that turns existing CCTV footage into customer-engagement, staff-performance, and store-traffic analytics without new cameras and without a dedicated GPU.

RetailPulse is organised as a multi-agent pipeline orchestrated with LangGraph. Detection, Classification, Engagement, and Trajectory agents process a three-camera setup (one entrance camera, two floor cameras). An Orchestrator reconciles their outputs per tracked entity, and an Insight Narration agent produces natural-language summaries on demand. The floor-distance prototype uses a trained non-biometric employee/customer detector, while earlier demo paths use zone and appearance heuristics. A Next.js dashboard serves managers and employees, and a FastAPI and SQLite service provides point-of-sale (POS) analytics. An experimental, CPU-oriented camera-handoff pipeline associates people leaving one camera with people entering the next using YOLO detection, ByteTrack identifiers, and lightweight appearance descriptors.

The project follows an iterative approach. At the FYP-1 mid-report stage the web application with manager and employee roles, the POS analytics service, and the camera-handoff MVP are implemented. A separate floor-distance CLI reuses trained role detections and ByteTrack, maps supported box-bottom proxies through a provisional floor homography, and rejects unverified camera alignment. Seventeen focused software checks and four existing handoff checks passed. The dashboard vision agents and orchestration are partly represented by prototype data. Early validation is described in Chapter~\ref{ch:testing}. FYP-2 will integrate the vision agents with the dashboard, evaluate them on the supplied store videos, and harden the system for a pilot-style demonstration.
